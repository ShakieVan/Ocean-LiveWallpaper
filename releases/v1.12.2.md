# Ocean 1.12.2 (Beta) — Initial-only horizon alignment, gyroscope takeover in flat posture, and longitudinal-tilt decoupling

Ocean 1.12.2 (Beta) refines the 3rd gyroscope axis (**Rotate screen (3rd axis)**) so that spirit-level vs. flat-screen horizon alignment occurs strictly at initialization (when the wallpaper becomes visible / resets), and the 3D gyroscope takes over afterward in every device posture without coupling into longitudinal (Y-axis) tilts:

- **Horizon alignment strictly at initialization (`!calibrated || screenRotation != calibratedRotation`):**
  - When the wallpaper becomes visible and resets its baseline, `initialRoll = horizonRoll(remapped)` is determined once: aligning the horizon like a **spirit level** (*Wasserwaage*) when the phone is held upright or angled, and aligning the horizon **with the screen (`0 rad`)** when the phone lies nearly flat ($\sqrt{g_x^2 + g_y^2} \le 0.16$).
  - Immediately after initialization, the gyroscope takes over (`roll = wrapRadians(initialRoll + deflectionRoll(relative))`).
- **Full 3rd-axis rotation when flat & tilt without unwanted rotation:**
  - When initialized while lying flat (`initialRoll = 0`), rotating the phone in its plane now rotates the ocean 1:1 with the gyroscope (`deflectionRoll(relative)`).
  - Tilting the phone slightly from a flat initialized state keeps `deflectionRoll(relative) = 0`, so the view does not rotate when tilting the flat device.
- **Zero coupling when tilting around the longitudinal axis (*Längsachse*):**
  - Instead of recomputing gravity roll every frame (which coupled longitudinal Y-axis tilts into Z-roll whenever the phone was held at $\theta < 90^\circ$), `deflectionRoll(relative)` extracts the Z-rotation from the projection of the phone's longitudinal axis (column 1 of `relative`: $\operatorname{atan2}(-\text{relative}[1], \text{relative}[4])$) onto the initialized baseline plane. Because right-multiplying by $\mathbf{R}_y(\beta)$ leaves column 1 of `relative` identically unchanged, tilting the phone around its longitudinal axis produces zero horizon tilt.
