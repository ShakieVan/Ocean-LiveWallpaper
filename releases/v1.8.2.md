# Ocean 1.8.2 — Calmer caustics with adjustable speed

The new **Settings > Caustic speed** (`Kaustik-Geschwindigkeit`) slider controls
the movement of the optional water light and its reflections. **100%** matches
the previous speed, **40%** is the calmer default, and **0%** freezes the wave
pattern. Fish and plants keep moving independently. Adjusting the slider
preserves the current light phase instead of jumping to another pattern.

Caustic bands now vary in width and sharpness across the scene and over time.
Broader, softer patches alternate with locally sharper bright edges. The
distance-softened shadows keep their existing behaviour.

Enable **Moving water light** (`Bewegtes Wasserlicht`) to use the effect.
Existing settings and switch positions are preserved; water light and soft
shadows still default to off. The new speed defaults to 40% when updating.
This remains an approximation without ray tracing. Phone frame-rate and
battery impact have not been measured.

[Watch the native Android preview at 40% speed](https://github.com/ShakieVan/Ocean-LiveWallpaper/blob/main/media/ocean-water-light-182.mp4).
The 3.2-second clip contains 48 native emulator test frames sampled at 15 time
points per second, with fixed fish poses and camera. Plants advance at normal
speed; the water light runs at 40%. This is not a device-FPS measurement.

Native Android checks passed for the speed control, light field, surface
recreation and repeated visibility changes. Version **1.8.2 / code 30**.
Update through **Settings > Updates**, or download the universal APK from the
[1.8.2 release](https://github.com/ShakieVan/Ocean-LiveWallpaper/releases/tag/v1.8.2).

Release verification: four system previews beside an active wallpaper and
screen resume passed with both effects enabled in a freshly started API-36
emulator. Two earlier runs stalled in the emulator graphics path and Android
terminated the app as unresponsive. Restarting the complete emulator process
allowed the unchanged APK to pass; an Android-only reboot had not helped.
The underlying cause remains unresolved. This is not a phone stability test.
