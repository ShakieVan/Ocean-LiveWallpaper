# Ocean 1.10.0 — Natural Actio=Reactio fish biomechanics, underwater hydrodynamics, visible marine snow and lighter per-frame rendering

Ocean 1.10.0 brings natural fin-driven fish swimming and gliding, underwater fluid mechanics, configurable marine snow, optical detail refinements, lighter per-frame GPU/CPU rendering work, and Samsung Galaxy S24 Ultra battery-meter support for recent firmware updates (keeping all coral and fish fluorescence patterns and intensities untouched):

- **Actio = Reactio fish biomechanics & continuous gliding:**
  - **Fin-first propulsion and steering:** tail and pectoral fin activity ramps up ahead of body acceleration and course changes, then relaxes smoothly as the fish transitions into an inertial forward glide without stop-and-go motion.
  - **Center-of-mass spine pivot (no inward curve drift):** spine bending pivots around the fish's center of mass rather than the snout tip, eliminating inward sideways drift during turns while keeping scalar spine curvature cleanly separated from tail-tip height so sharp turns never distort the tail fin.
  - **Active asymmetric turn strokes:** course changes initiate with a preparatory C-turn impulse and asymmetric tail power strokes on the outside of the turn rather than holding the tail fin sideways like a static rudder.
  - **Labriform 3D pectoral-fin propulsion:** reef fish actively row with their pectoral fins during normal cruising and acceleration (synchronously in phase when swimming straight using a 3D figure-8 feathering and downward/backward power stroke, and differentially in turns where the outer fin rows wider and the inner fin flares as a pivot brake).
  - **Passive hydrodynamic dorsal pennant (*Heniochus*):** the Pennant Coralfish's long white dorsal filament is decoupled from active tail-fin propulsion; it erects slightly during slow swimming or hovering and lays flatter backward under water drag at higher speeds.
- **Underwater hydrodynamics, optical details & visible marine snow:**
  - **Traveling-wave fin flex & reef currents:** progressive carangiform S-wave with taut leading fin rays and water-resistance trailing membrane flex, orbital reef wave swell, smooth collision-checked coral wake eddies, speed-coupled banking in turns, gentle buoyancy sway while hovering, and subtle gill-cover breathing. Adjustable under **Fish & Corals > Current & fluid mechanics** (`0–150%`, default `100%`).
  - **Visible marine snow / drifting particles:** sunlit Tyndall-scattering motes with orbital wave surge and turbulent eddy drift. Adjustable under **Appearance > Drifting particles** (`0–200%`, default `100%`; `0%` disables the particle layer completely).
  - **Underwater optical details:** warm subsurface translucency on backlit fins and anemone/plant tips, wet cornea gloss on fish eyes, subtle scale shimmer in turns, and natural depth colour absorption on distant fish (reflected light only, leaving fluorescence untouched).
- **Lighter per-frame GPU/CPU work & Galaxy S24 Ultra battery meter:**
  - **Lighter dynamic shadow cascades alongside static reef shadows:** when precomputed `2048×2048` EVSM coral shadows are active, the dynamic cascades (holding moving fish and swaying plants) use `1024×1024` contact-hardened `DPCF` instead of running 66 full-screen `2048×2048` `RGBA16F` Gaussian blur passes per frame, and skip near-zero triplanar detail fetches on coral faces.
  - **Lighter water-light & per-fish work:** moving caustics reuse the already computed wave curvature and pixel footprint; downward-facing reflections and unshadowed static-shadow pixels skip redundant lookups; background light shafts use a precomputed trigonometric table (4 instead of 432 sine/cosine evaluations per frame); fish transforms are built directly without per-axis JNI matrix calls; and fish scale tangent frames are skipped on head, eye and fin pixels.
  - **Galaxy S24 Ultra battery meter:** recognises the `S928BXXS6DZI1` firmware update and subsequent `SM-S928B` release builds so milliampere current readings are converted to microamperes as before.

All existing settings, frame-rate choices (30, 40, 50 and 60 FPS), fish counts and coral layouts remain unchanged. **Phone battery savings, frame rate and temperature have not been measured.**
